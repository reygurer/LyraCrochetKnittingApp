# How to Reach and Use Your Personalized Pattern App

Welcome! This app takes your own swatch and body measurements and turns them into a pattern that's actually sized for you, not a generic S/M/L. Here's exactly how to get in and how to use everything inside.

## Getting into the app

When you bought your pattern on Etsy, you should have received two things in your digital download: a link to the app and an access key for your specific pattern. Neither of these is a file you need to install. You just open the link in your browser, the same way you'd open any website.

The first time you visit, you'll land on a simple sign in screen. There's no password to remember. Just type in the same email address you used when you checked out on Etsy, and click Continue. The app uses your email to recognize you and remember which patterns you've unlocked, so it's important to use that exact email.

Once you're signed in, you'll see your library, a list of all the patterns in the shop.

A small tip: after you sign in, your browser's address bar will show your email tagged onto the link. If you bookmark that page, you can come back anytime without typing your email again.

## Unlocking your pattern

In your library, find the pattern you bought. If it's free, you can open it right away. If it's a paid pattern, you'll see a small "Unlock with a key" section. Open that and paste in the access key from your Etsy order, then click Verify.

Two things need to line up for this to work: the key has to match your pattern, and your email has to be on file as having actually bought it. If you get an error saying there's no order on file, double check that you're signed in with the exact email you used on Etsy. If everything matches but you're still stuck, that usually means the order hasn't been added to the system yet, so it's worth reaching out to the shop directly.

Once you unlock a pattern successfully, that's it, it's yours for good. You won't need to enter the key again, even if you come back weeks later or switch to a different phone or laptop. Just sign in with the same email and your pattern will already be sitting there unlocked in your library.

## Before you start: understanding your gauge

Gauge is just a fancy word for how many stitches and rows fit into a set measurement, usually 10cm. It matters because everybody knits or crochets a little differently, some people pull tighter, some looser, and the size of your finished project depends entirely on this number. That's why the app asks for your own swatch measurements instead of assuming everyone's gauge matches the pattern designer's.

To get an accurate gauge, knit or crochet a small swatch using the yarn and hook or needle size you plan to use for the actual project. Measure it twice: once while it's just resting flat and relaxed, and once while you gently stretch it to the tension it would sit at when worn. Fabric behaves differently under wear than it does lying still, so having both numbers gives a much more honest picture than just one measurement.

## Filling in the pattern form

Once you open your pattern, you'll walk through a short form. It's split into a few natural groups:

**Swatch measurements**, where you enter the stitch count, row count, and the width and height of your swatch in centimeters. This is what the app uses to calculate your real gauge.

**Yarn info**, where you note down details about the yarn you're using, so the yarn amount estimate can be tailored to it.

**Body measurements**, where you enter the actual measurements the pattern needs from you, like chest width, length, sleeve length, or whatever that specific pattern calls for.

You don't need to fill everything in at once if you're not sure of a number yet. You can always come back and adjust a field, then recalculate.

Once everything looks right, click "Calculate my numbers."

## Getting your results

After you calculate, the app shows your personal stitch and row counts laid out clearly, along with a rough total yarn estimate in meters and grams if the pattern includes that. If the pattern has written instructions, you'll see them filled in with your actual numbers rather than generic placeholders, so you know exactly how many stitches to cast on or how many rows to work before decreasing.

If you want to change a measurement, click "Edit measurements" to go back to the form, adjust anything, and recalculate as many times as you like.

When you're happy with your numbers, click "Download as Excel" to save a spreadsheet with your inputs and results on separate sheets. It's yours to keep, print, or refer back to while you're working on your project.

## If your pattern has a video

Some patterns come with a walkthrough video from the maker. If yours does, you'll see two tabs after you unlock it: "Watch video" for the video walkthrough, and "Written pattern & measurements" for the written form described above. You can watch the video, fill out the form, or do both, whichever helps you more.

## The extra tools

Alongside your patterns, the app includes a few standalone calculators you can use anytime, even without a specific pattern open.

**Gauge Converter** lets you check your gauge on its own. Enter your stitch and row count along with your relaxed and stretched swatch measurements, and it'll give you stitches and rows per 10cm for both states, plus an average, which is usually the most realistic number to design from. If your gauge doesn't match what a pattern calls for, you can enter your current needle size and your target gauge, and it'll suggest a new needle size to try.

**Yarn Calculator** gives you a rough, general estimate of how much yarn a project might need, based on the type of project, the size, and the yarn weight you're using. It also lets you check whether the yarn you already have on hand is likely to be enough, just enter how many skeins you have and how many meters are in each one.

**Pattern Scaler** is handy when you already have a stitch or row count from somewhere and need to adjust it for a different gauge, a different size, or to round it to a specific multiple (useful for stitch patterns that repeat in groups). Enter the original count and gauge, your gauge, and any multiple you need to hit, and it recalculates the count for you.

**Row Counter** helps you keep track of where you are while you're actually working. Add a project with a name and, if you know it, a total row count. From there you can tap +1 or −1 as you work through rows, reset back to zero if you need to, and jot down a note for yourself, like where you paused or what you need to remember for next time. You can track several projects at once, which is handy if you've got more than one thing on the needles.

## A quick recap

Open your link, sign in with your Etsy email, unlock your pattern with your key, fill in your swatch and body measurements, calculate, and download your Excel sheet. Bookmark the page so you never have to type your email in again, and remember your extra tools, the Gauge Converter, Yarn Calculator, Pattern Scaler, and Row Counter, are always there whenever you need them, pattern or no pattern.

If anything doesn't work the way it's described here, the most common fix is making sure you're signed in with the exact email you used at checkout. Beyond that, feel free to reach out to the shop for help.
